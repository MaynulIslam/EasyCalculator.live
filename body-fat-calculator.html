US Navy Body Fat Method

The US Navy method uses body circumference measurements to estimate body fat percentage. It was developed by Hodgdon and Beckett at the Naval Health Research Center.

Male (inches): BF% = 86.010 × log₁₀(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log₁₀(height) + 36.76

Female (inches): BF% = 163.205 × log₁₀(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log₁₀(height) − 78.387

How to Measure Correctly

  • Height: Stand straight without shoes, measure to the top of your head.
  • Neck: Measure just below the larynx (Adam's apple), sloping downward to the front.
  • Waist (Male): Measure at the navel level.
  • Waist (Female): Measure at the narrowest point, usually above the navel.
  • Hips (Female): Measure at the widest point below the waist.

Worked Example

A 6'0" (183 cm) male measures: neck = 16" (40.6 cm), waist = 34" (86.4 cm). Plugging into the formula: BF% = 86.010 × log₁₀(34 − 16) − 70.041 × log₁₀(72) + 36.76 = 86.010 × log₁₀(18) − 70.041 × log₁₀(72) + 36.76 ≈ 86.010 × 1.2553 − 70.041 × 1.8573 + 36.76 ≈ 107.95 − 130.07 + 36.76 ≈ 14.6% — solidly in the Fitness category.

Accuracy vs. Other Methods

The US Navy method is accurate to within ±3–4 percentage points for most people. Compare that to DEXA scan (X-ray bone density scan, ±1–2%, gold standard but expensive), hydrostatic weighing (underwater weighing, ±1.5–3%, lab setting required), skinfold calipers (±3–4%, requires trained technician), and simple BMI (no fat measurement at all). The Navy method's main advantage is that it requires only a tape measure and is repeatable at home — making it ideal for tracking trends over time.

Visceral vs. Subcutaneous Fat

Subcutaneous fat sits just under your skin — it's the fat you can pinch. While excess subcutaneous fat affects appearance and can increase health risk at very high levels, it is far less dangerous than visceral fat, which wraps around your internal organs. Visceral fat is strongly linked to insulin resistance, Type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and inflammation. A high waist circumference (above 40" for men, 35" for women) is the primary warning sign of excess visceral fat. The Navy method estimates total body fat, not visceral fat specifically — a DEXA scan or MRI can distinguish between the two.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy body fat percentage? +

Healthy ranges differ by sex. For men: 6–13% is athletic, 14–17% is fitness level, 18–24% is acceptable, and 25%+ is obese. For women: 14–20% athletic, 21–24% fitness, 25–31% acceptable, 32%+ obese. Essential fat (the minimum for survival) is 2–5% for men and 10–13% for women.

How accurate is the US Navy body fat calculator? +

The US Navy method is accurate to within ±3–4 percentage points compared to DEXA scan (gold standard at ±1–2%). It is significantly more accurate than BMI for assessing body composition because it accounts for body shape, not just height and weight. Consistency matters most — take measurements under the same conditions each time to reliably track changes.

Where exactly do I measure for the body fat formula? +

Neck: just below the larynx (Adam's apple), angled slightly downward at the front. Waist (men): at the navel. Waist (women): at the narrowest point, usually above the navel. Hips (women only): at the widest point of the buttocks. Take each measurement twice and average them. Use a flexible tape measure held snug but not compressing the skin.

What is the difference between subcutaneous and visceral fat? +

Subcutaneous fat sits under the skin and is the type you can pinch. Visceral fat surrounds your internal organs inside the abdominal cavity. Visceral fat is far more dangerous — it is directly linked to insulin resistance, Type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and chronic inflammation. A waist circumference above 40" (men) or 35" (women) is a key warning sign of excess visceral fat.

Is body fat percentage better than BMI? +

Yes, for most purposes. Body fat percentage directly measures the proportion of fat in your body, distinguishing it from lean mass (muscle, bone, organs). BMI only uses height and weight, so a muscular athlete can be flagged as "overweight" or "obese" by BMI despite having low body fat. Use both metrics together for a fuller picture.

Can I build muscle and lose fat at the same time? +

Yes — this is called body recomposition, and it works best for beginners, those returning after a break, or people with higher body fat percentages. Eat near maintenance calories, train with progressive resistance, and consume 0.7–1g of protein per pound of bodyweight. Advanced athletes find simultaneous muscle gain and fat loss harder and typically alternate between dedicated bulk and cut phases.

What body fat percentage shows visible abs? +

Most men can see defined abs at 10–12% body fat. At 14–15% there may be a faint outline with good lighting. For women, visible abs typically appear around 16–19% due to naturally higher essential fat levels. Genetics, abdominal muscle development, and water retention all affect how defined abs look at any given body fat percentage.

How often should I measure my body fat? +

Once every 2–4 weeks is ideal for tracking progress without obsessing over short-term fluctuations. Body fat doesn't change significantly day-to-day, and daily measurements are influenced heavily by water retention and meal timing. Always measure at the same time of day (morning, before eating or drinking) under the same conditions for the most consistent results.
